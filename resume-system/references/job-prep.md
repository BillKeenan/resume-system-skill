# Job Prep (companion to the resume build)

For senior roles, the resume earns the screen; preparation decides the outcome. Run this once the posting exists. Save as `job-targets/[company]-[role-slug]-prep.md`. The file should grow after every round, not stay static.

## 1. Company & role research
Product and market position; recent news; **why the role is open** (growth, backfill, reorg, new function; "unknown" is a valid answer); where the role sits and who else is on the leadership team; what success looks like in the first 90 days.

## 2. Contacts & relationships
Hiring manager (background, priorities, style); existing connections and whether each can act as a reference or only as context; interview panel (name, role, likely focus), filled in as scheduled.

## 3. Story bank
Map accomplishments from the role files to likely themes, using the posting's primary axis to choose which themes matter most. One or two strongest stories per theme in shorthand:

| Theme | Story | Situation/Task | Action | Result (metric) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Seed themes for senior leaders: scale and org complexity; transformation/turnaround; product and business partnership; technical judgment; hiring and talent development; difficult personnel decisions; conflict with peers or execs; failure and recovery; communicating to boards or executive teams.

### 3a. Depth readiness (when the role expects technical credibility)
For each technical story, decide in advance, using the depth tiers in `honesty-rules.md`, whether it is Tier 1, 2 or 3, and write the pivot line. Default to the lower tier until confirmed.

### 3b. Technology study plan (when the role expects current stack fluency)
List every technology across the role files and prioritize:
- **Tier 1 — role-critical:** what the target company runs or the disciplines the role will lead. Study to architecture-level fluency.
- **Tier 2 — positioning-critical:** supports the resume's primary axis.
- **Tier 3 — history refresh:** older technologies, only if a past role is likely to be probed.
The goal is informed conversation, not mastery, and it must stay consistent with the honest depth framing.

## 4. Gaps & risk mitigation
For each gap from Phase 1, choose **reframe** (adjacent experience covers it) or **acknowledge and pivot** (own it briefly, redirect to a strength). Decide before the interview.

## 5. Questions to ask them
3–5 questions showing real insight, drawn from the company research, the reason the role is open, and how year-one success will be measured. Avoid generic ones.

## 6. Comp & negotiation
Target range (base, bonus, equity), walk-away point, leverage (competing offers, unique fit), and company-specific norms. Don't over-invest until an offer is realistic.

## Using the file
1. §1–2 as soon as the posting exists.
2. §3 right after Phase 2 of the resume build; §3a/3b only when the role calls for them.
3. §4–5 before the first screen.
4. §6 when an offer is realistically close.
5. After each round, update the panel table and add debrief notes to §3.
