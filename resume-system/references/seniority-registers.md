# Seniority Registers

Bullet voice by level. Pick the register of the **target** role, but never claim scope the user didn't have.

## Director
- Owns delivery and team health for one or more teams (typically 20–80 people, with managers reporting in).
- Voice: execution with accountability. "Delivered", "stood up", "improved predictability from X to Y".
- Emphasis: process, predictability, hiring, partnering with product, stakeholder management.

## VP
- Owns an engineering function or business-aligned org; manages managers; shapes operating model.
- Voice: transformation and org design. "Redesigned the operating model", "built the leadership bench", "partnered with executive team".
- Emphasis: org structure, career ladders, hiring engine, cross-functional influence, cost and efficiency.

## SVP
- Owns multiple orgs or businesses, often across geographies; accountable at executive-team level.
- Voice: scale and strategy. "Led ~N staff across M countries", "set technical and organizational strategy for…".
- Emphasis: scale, multi-business ownership, M&A/partnerships, board/exec communication, P&L or budget.

## CTO / C-level
- Owns technology strategy for the company; external-facing (board, investors, customers).
- Voice: company outcomes. Revenue, valuation, platform bets, fundraising, product-market fit.

## Universal rules
- Lead with business impact and scale; technology supports credibility.
- Every bullet: `[Action verb] + [what] + [resulting in] + [quantified outcome or scale signal]`.
- **Strong verbs:** led, built, scaled, transformed, launched, restructured, partnered, drove, established.
- **Failure signs:** "responsible for", "helped", "assisted", "contributed to" (unless that is the honest tier, since honesty outranks register), tool lists instead of outcomes, activity without result ("worked with product to…").
- Show the career arc: the reader should see each step building toward the target role.
- Step-down titles (e.g. targeting a VP role as a current SVP) are fine: frame the posting's scope as the draw, not the title.
